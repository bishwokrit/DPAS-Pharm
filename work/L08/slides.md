## Slide 5: Combined Hormonal Contraception
- [heading] ESTROGENS, PROGESTINS, AND CONTRACEPTION
- [heading] Combined Hormonal Contraception
- **Mechanism: estrogen + progestin suppress GnRH/FSH/LH, preventing ovulation (Figure 11); progestin also thickens cervical mucus and thins the endometrium.**
- Available as oral pills, transdermal patch, or vaginal ring.
- **Contraindications: history of VTE/stroke, estrogen-sensitive cancer, uncontrolled hypertension, migraine with aura, and smokers age 35 or older (increased thromboembolic risk).**
- Progestin-only options (e.g., the "minipill," depot medroxyprogesterone, levonorgestrel IUD) avoid estrogen-related thromboembolic risk and are preferred when estrogen is contraindicated.

## Slide 7: SERMs and Androgen Pharmacology
- [heading] SELECTIVE MODULATION
- [heading] SERMs and Androgen Pharmacology
- [heading] Selective Estrogen-Receptor Modulators (SERMs)
- [heading] Androgens & Antiandrogens
- Tamoxifen — antagonist in breast tissue (breast cancer treatment/prevention), agonist in endometrium (raises endometrial cancer risk)
- Raloxifene — antagonist in breast/endometrium, agonist in bone (osteoporosis prevention) — ties back to Unit 2 bone pharmacology

- Finasteride — 5-α-reductase inhibitor; reduces conversion of testosterone to DHT (BPH, androgenetic alopecia)
- Spironolactone — antiandrogen effect at high doses; used off-label for hirsutism/acne (same drug as the mineralocorticoid antagonist from Session 1)

## Slide 12: Insulin Therapy
- [heading] EXOGENOUS REPLACEMENT
- [heading] Insulin Therapy
- **Mechanism: exogenous insulin activates the insulin receptor, promoting cellular glucose uptake and suppressing hepatic gluconeogenesis.**
- Rapid-acting (lispro, aspart) — mealtime dosing; onset ~15 minutes.
- Short-acting (regular) — onset ~30 minutes.
- Intermediate-acting (NPH) — cloudy suspension; more variable absorption.
- Long-acting (glargine, detemir, degludec) — basal coverage; relatively peakless profile.
- **Primary adverse effect across all insulins: hypoglycemia — counsel patients on recognition and treatment (rapid-acting carbohydrate).**
- **14**

_[1 small image(s)/icons]_

Instructor script:

## Slide 21: Choosing a Second Agent for Type 2 Diabetes

- [heading] Choosing a Second Agent for Type 2 Diabetes

## Slide 25: Hyperparathyroidism vs. Hypoparathyroidism Therapy
- [heading] OPPOSITE PROBLEMS
- [heading] Hyperparathyroidism vs. Hypoparathyroidism Therapy
- [heading] Hyperparathyroidism
- [heading] Hypoparathyroidism
- Cinacalcet — a calcimimetic; activates the calcium-sensing receptor on parathyroid cells, reducing PTH secretion
- Used for secondary hyperparathyroidism (e.g., in chronic kidney disease) and parathyroid carcinoma
- Monitor for hypocalcemia with therapy
- Calcitriol (active vitamin D) plus oral calcium — mainstay of therapy, since PTH deficiency impairs vitamin D activation
- Recombinant human PTH 1-84 — reserved for patients not adequately controlled on calcium/calcitriol alone
- Monitor for hypercalcemia/hypercalciuria with therapy

## Slide 27: Distinguishing the Two Parathyroid Disorders

- [heading] Key Takeaways

## Slide 35: Lecture Recall Quiz — Answer Key
- [heading] ANSWER KEY
- [heading] Lecture Recall Quiz — Answer Key
- **1.  B —** **PTU blocks both new thyroid hormone synthesis and peripheral T4-to-T3 conversion, giving it a faster effect on circulating active hormone than methimazole — important in the acute setting of thyroid storm.**
- **2.  B —** **Chronic exogenous glucocorticoid exposure suppresses CRH/ACTH via negative feedback (Figure 9), reducing the adrenal cortex’s stimulus to produce cortisol; abrupt withdrawal does not give the axis time to recover, precipitating adrenal insufficiency.**
- **3.  B —** **Smoking at age 35 or older is a well-established contraindication to estrogen-containing contraception due to substantially increased thromboembolic and cardiovascular risk.**
- **4.  A —** **Sulfonylureas bind the K-ATP channel directly and force closure regardless of glucose level (Figure 15), while incretin-based agents amplify insulin release only when glucose is already elevated — explaining the difference in hypoglycemia risk.**
- **5.  B —** **Because PTH is required to activate vitamin D, hypoparathyroidism impairs that activation step; calcitriol (already-active vitamin D) bypasses it, and is given together with oral calcium as first-line therapy.**
- **6.  B —** **SGLT2 inhibitors block renal glucose reabsorption, increasing urinary glucose concentration and creating a favorable environment for genital fungal overgrowth — a mechanism-based, well-known adverse effect.**
