## Slide 18: Rumack-Matthew Nomogram: Assessing Toxicity
- **MECHANISM & DEPTH**
- [heading] Rumack-Matthew Nomogram: Assessing Toxicity
- Illustrative schematic, single acute ingestion.
- **How to use:**  draw a level at 4 hours or later after a single acute ingestion and plot it against time since ingestion.
- **Above the treatment line:**  give NAC.
- **Below the line:**  toxicity unlikely.
- **Limits:**  not valid before 4 hours, for unknown ingestion time, staggered or repeated supratherapeutic ingestion, or chronic use; treat these using clinical judgment, AST/ALT and poison control.
- Example: 8 h, 120 mcg/mL is above the line, so start NAC.

_[figure(s) saved: S18_1.jpg]_

> Speaker notes: The nomogram predicts hepatotoxicity risk after a single acute ingestion; if time of ingestion is unknown or ingestion is chronic, do not rely on it. Start NAC without waiting if presentation is late or the level is unavailable and ingestion is significant.

After 24 hours the nomogram is not used: 4.7 mcg/mL is only where the treatment line ends, not a safe cutoff. Get AST/ALT; a detectable level or elevated AST/ALT guides NAC, per protocol or poison control.

## Slide 29: COX-2 Selective Inhibition: Celecoxib
- [heading] SELECTIVE AGENT
- [heading] COX-2 Selective Inhibition: Celecoxib
- **Celecoxib selectively inhibits COX-2 over COX-1.**
- GI effects: lower risk of ulcer/bleeding than nonselective NSAIDs.
- **Cardiovascular risk: COX-2 selectivity is associated with increased cardiovascular event risk — a key trade-off.**
- Renal effects: similar renal risk to nonselective NSAIDs — COX-2 selectivity does not protect the kidney.

_[1 small image(s)/icons]_

> Speaker notes: This is a classic "trade one risk for another" teaching point — better GI profile, but not a free lunch cardiovascularly or renally.

Know (interactions): NSAID plus anticoagulant means more bleeding. NSAID plus ACE inhibitor/ARB plus diuretic is the "triple whammy" for AKI. NSAID plus corticosteroid means more GI ulcer risk.

## Slide 52: Biologic DMARDs: Mechanism by Target
