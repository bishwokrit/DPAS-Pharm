## Slide 7: GERD, Peptic Ulcer Disease, and Gastritis
- [heading] CLINICAL SPECTRUM
- [heading] GERD, Peptic Ulcer Disease, and Gastritis
- **●  GERD:** **reflux of gastric contents into the esophagus, causing heartburn/regurgitation and, over time, esophageal mucosal injury.**
- **●  Peptic ulcer disease (PUD):** **a break in the mucosa of the stomach or duodenum; most commonly caused by H. pylori infection or NSAID use.**
- **●  Gastritis:** **inflammation of the gastric mucosa, which can be acute (e.g., NSAID- or alcohol-related) or chronic (e.g., H. pylori-driven).**

## Slide 15: Proton Pump Inhibitors (PPIs)
- [heading] MOST POTENT ACID SUPPRESSION
- [heading] Proton Pump Inhibitors (PPIs)
- **Mechanism: irreversibly inhibit the H⁺/K⁺-ATPase proton pump — the final common pathway of acid secretion.**
- **Omeprazole, esomeprazole, pantoprazole, lansoprazole, dexlansoprazole — first-line for GERD, PUD healing, and H. pylori regimens.**
- **Best absorbed/effective when taken 30–60 minutes before a meal (timed to coincide with peak pump activity).**
- **Long-term adverse effects: vitamin B12 deficiency, hypomagnesemia, increased fracture risk, and increased risk of Clostridioides difficile infection.**
- **Interaction: omeprazole (a CYP2C19 inhibitor) can reduce the activation of clopidogrel, potentially blunting its antiplatelet effect.**
- **Abrupt discontinuation after prolonged use can cause rebound acid hypersecretion.**
- **PPI prodrug activated by acid**
- **▼**
- **Binds H⁺/K⁺ ATPase irreversibly**
- **▼**
- **Final step of acid secretion blocked**
- **▼**
- [heading] Profound, prolonged acid suppression

_[1 small image(s)/icons]_

> Speaker notes: The long-term adverse-effect list is high-yield for both exams and real-world deprescribing conversations — many patients are on PPIs far longer than clinically necessary.

Instructor script:

Know:

Take 30-60 minutes before the first meal, because PPIs work on actively secreting pumps.

Long-term risks: C. difficile infection, fractures, hypomagnesemia, B12 deficiency, pneumonia, acute interstitial nephritis, and rebound acid hypersecretion on stopping.

Omeprazole and esomeprazole inhibit CYP2C19 and can blunt clopidogrel activation.

CYP2C19 genotype changes PPI exposure. Ultrarapid metabolizers may need higher doses.

## Slide 23: H. pylori Eradication Therapy
- [heading] TREATING THE CAUSE
- [heading] H. pylori Eradication Therapy
- **H. pylori is the leading identifiable cause of peptic ulcer disease — eradication, not just acid suppression, is the goal.**
- **Triple therapy: PPI + amoxicillin + clarithromycin for 10–14 days.**
- **Bismuth quadruple therapy: PPI (or H2RA) + bismuth subsalicylate + metronidazole + tetracycline — preferred where clarithromycin resistance is a concern or with recent macrolide exposure.**
- **Penicillin allergy: substitute metronidazole for amoxicillin in triple therapy.**
- **Confirm eradication with testing (e.g., urea breath test or stool antigen) after completing therapy, not during PPI therapy, which can cause false negatives.**

_[1 small image(s)/icons]_

> Speaker notes: Multi-drug regimens like this are a classic source of adherence problems — worth pausing to ask students what patient-counseling points would most improve completion of a 10–14 day, 3–4 drug regimen.

Instructor script:

Know:

Usual duration is 14 days.

Bismuth quadruple therapy: PPI plus bismuth plus tetracycline plus metronidazole. It is preferred with penicillin allergy or prior macrolide exposure.

Confirm eradication at least 4 weeks after therapy and with the PPI held for 2 weeks.

Clarithromycin inhibits CYP3A4 (statins). Metronidazole plus alcohol causes a disulfiram-like reaction. Avoid tetracycline in pregnancy and in children.
