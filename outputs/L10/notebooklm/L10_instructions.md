# NotebookLM instructions — L10: Renal Pharmacotherapeutics — Session 2

## How to use
1. In NotebookLM, create a new notebook named "L10 Renal Pharmacotherapeutics — Session 2".
2. Add sources: upload every `L10_source*.md` file in this folder (nothing else).
3. For each feature below, click its **Customize** (pencil) option and paste the box under it.

## 3. Quiz and flashcards
```
Use ONLY the uploaded source.
Quiz: PANCE-style. Mix about half clinical vignettes and half mechanism questions. Five answer choices (A–E), one best answer, all choices similar in length and detail. Explain why the right answer is right and why each wrong choice is wrong. Cover every topic and every learning objective; weight lecture-emphasized (bold) facts more heavily.
Flashcards: one fact per card. Front = a question or cue (drug, class, effect, or "how do you tell X from Y"). Back = the answer with its citation (slide or page). Include mechanism-to-side-effect "why" cards and discriminator cards.
```

## 4. Questions to ask the notebook
1. Walk the RAAS cascade and name the drug that blocks each step.
2. Why are ACE inhibitors and ARBs first-line in proteinuric CKD, and why does creatinine rise?
3. When do you switch an ACE inhibitor to an ARB, and what about aliskiren?
4. Compare dihydropyridine and non-dihydropyridine CCBs.
5. How do nitrates work, and why never with sildenafil?
6. Explain the triple whammy and how to prevent it.

Learning objectives for reference:
- IO5.1: Identify major pharmacologic classes used in the management of renal conditions and describe their mechanisms of action.
- IO5.2: Describe the indications, contraindications, adverse effects, and clinically significant interactions associated with medications used to treat renal conditions.
- IO5.3: Compare pharmacologic agents within and across therapeutic classes to distinguish clinically relevant differences in efficacy, safety, and use.
- IO5.4: Select appropriate pharmacologic therapy for common renal conditions based on clinical presentation and patient-specific factors.
- IO5.5: Identify appropriate dosing, administration, and monitoring considerations for medications used in renal conditions.
- IO5.6: Explain important medication-use and safety information to patients receiving pharmacotherapy for renal.
- SO1: Describe the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system (RAAS) and identify where each antihypertensive drug class acts.
- SO2: Explain why ACE inhibitors and ARBs are preferred first-line therapy for hypertension in chronic kidney disease, including their renoprotective mechanism.
- SO3: Identify major antihypertensive drug classes beyond RAAS blockers, their mechanisms, and their role in CKD-specific management.
- SO4: Describe vasodilator pharmacology (nitrates, calcium channel blockers) in angina pectoris and its mechanistic overlap with cardiovascular-renal disease management.
- SO5: Identify high-risk drug combinations and monitoring needs at the cardiorenal interface, including the "triple whammy," and apply the full renal/cardiorenal toolkit to integrated clinical scenarios with a cumulative recall quiz.
