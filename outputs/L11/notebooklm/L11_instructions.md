# NotebookLM instructions — L11: Genitourinary & Reproductive Pharmacology — Session 1

## How to use
1. In NotebookLM, create a new notebook named "L11 Genitourinary & Reproductive Pharmacology — Session 1".
2. Add sources: upload every `L11_source*.md` file in this folder (nothing else).
3. For each feature below, click its **Customize** (pencil) option and paste the box under it.

## 3. Quiz and flashcards
```
Use ONLY the uploaded source.
Quiz: PANCE-style. Mix about half clinical vignettes and half mechanism questions. Five answer choices (A–E), one best answer, all choices similar in length and detail. Explain why the right answer is right and why each wrong choice is wrong. Cover every topic and every learning objective; weight lecture-emphasized (bold) facts more heavily.
Flashcards: one fact per card. Front = a question or cue (drug, class, effect, or "how do you tell X from Y"). Back = the answer with its citation (slide or page). Include mechanism-to-side-effect "why" cards and discriminator cards.
```

## 4. Questions to ask the notebook
1. Why must a woman with a uterus get a progestin with estrogen?
2. Who should get a progestin-only method instead of a combined one?
3. Compare tamoxifen and raloxifene tissue by tissue.
4. Why are aromatase inhibitors postmenopausal drugs?
5. Where do finasteride, spironolactone and bicalutamide act on the androgen pathway?
6. Explain the PCOS–insulin resistance cycle and why metformin helps.

Learning objectives for reference:
- IO6.1: Identify major pharmacologic classes used in the management of genitourinary and reproductive conditions and describe their mechanisms of action.
- IO6.2: Describe the indications, contraindications, adverse effects, and clinically significant interactions associated with medications used to treat genitourinary and reproductive conditions.
- IO6.3: Compare pharmacologic agents within and across therapeutic classes to distinguish clinically relevant differences in efficacy, safety, and use.
- IO6.4: Select appropriate pharmacologic therapy for common genitourinary and reproductive conditions based on clinical presentation and patient-specific factors.
- IO6.5: Identify appropriate dosing, administration, and monitoring considerations for medications used in genitourinary and reproductive conditions.
- IO6.6: Explain important medication-use and safety information to patients receiving genitourinary and reproductive pharmacotherapy.
- SO1: Describe the physiologic roles and clinical uses of estrogens and progestins, including combined and progestin-only hormonal contraception.
- SO2: Describe the mechanism, tissue-selective actions, and adverse effects of selective estrogen receptor modulators (SERMs) and aromatase inhibitors.
- SO3: Describe the clinical uses, adverse effects, and monitoring considerations for androgens and antiandrogens.
- SO4: Explain the pathophysiology of polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS) as a reproductive-metabolic overlap condition, including the central role of insulin resistance.
- SO5: Describe the role of metformin and other antidiabetic agents in the management of PCOS and related reproductive-metabolic conditions.
