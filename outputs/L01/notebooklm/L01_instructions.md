## 3. Quiz and flashcards
```
Use ONLY the uploaded source.
Quiz: PANCE-style. Mix about half clinical vignettes and half mechanism questions. Five answer choices (A–E), one best answer, all choices similar in length and detail. Explain why the right answer is right and why each wrong choice is wrong. Cover every topic and every learning objective; weight lecture-emphasized (bold) facts more heavily.
Flashcards: one fact per card. Front = a question or cue (drug, class, effect, or "how do you tell X from Y"). Back = the answer with its citation (slide or page). Include mechanism-to-side-effect "why" cards and discriminator cards.
```

## 4. Questions to ask the notebook
1. What is the difference between pharmacokinetics and pharmacodynamics? Give one example of each from the lecture.
2. How do I tell a competitive antagonist from a noncompetitive antagonist on a dose-response curve?
3. Explain potency vs. efficacy, and why efficacy matters more clinically.
4. What does a narrow therapeutic index mean, and which four drugs are the classic examples?
5. Walk me through the first-pass effect and which routes avoid it.
6. Which factors decide whether a drug crosses the blood-brain barrier?
7. What is the difference between efficacy and effectiveness?
8. Quiz me on the four clinical trial phases: purpose and population.
9. How is an adverse drug reaction different from toxicity?

Learning objectives for reference:
- IO1.1: Define and describe fundamental pharmacologic concepts, including pharmacokinetics, pharmacodynamics, drug-receptor interactions, dose-response relationships, therapeutic index, half-life, and steady state.
- IO1.2: Describe the processes of drug absorption, distribution, metabolism, and excretion and factors that influence these processes.
- IO1.3: Explain how patient-specific factors, including age, pregnancy, renal and hepatic function, and pharmacogenetic variation, may affect medication response.
- IO1.4: Identify common types and mechanisms of adverse drug reactions, drug-drug interactions, and drug-disease interactions.
- IO1.5: Interpret common components of medication orders and prescriptions, including drug name, dose, route, frequency, and duration.
- SO1: Define pharmacology, pharmacodynamics, and pharmacokinetics.
- SO2: Describe the major stages of drug development and FDA approval.
- SO3: Explain how drugs interact with receptors to produce an effect.
- SO4: Interpret a dose-response curve, including potency and efficacy.
- SO5: Explain absorption, distribution, metabolism, and excretion (ADME), including bioavailability and the first-pass effect.
