## 3. Quiz and flashcards
```
Use ONLY the uploaded source.
Quiz: PANCE-style. Mix about half clinical vignettes and half mechanism questions. Five answer choices (A–E), one best answer, all choices similar in length and detail. Explain why the right answer is right and why each wrong choice is wrong. Cover every topic and every learning objective; weight lecture-emphasized (bold) facts more heavily.
```

Learning objectives for reference:
- IO4.1: Identify major pharmacologic classes used in the management of endocrine conditions and describe their mechanisms of action.
- IO4.2: Describe the indications, contraindications, adverse effects, and clinically significant interactions associated with medications used to treat endocrine conditions.
- IO4.3: Compare pharmacologic agents within and across therapeutic classes to distinguish clinically relevant differences in efficacy, safety, and use.
- IO4.4: Select appropriate pharmacologic therapy for common endocrine conditions based on clinical presentation and patient-specific factors.
- IO4.5: Identify appropriate dosing, administration, and monitoring considerations for medications used in endocrine conditions.
- IO4.6: Explain important medication-use and safety information to patients receiving pharmacotherapy for endocrine conditions.
- SO1: Describe the hypothalamic-pituitary-target gland axis as a shared regulatory template, and identify where drug therapy intervenes at each level.
- SO2: Identify the major agents used for growth hormone disorders, hyperprolactinemia, and disorders of antidiuretic hormone (diabetes insipidus, SIADH).
- SO3: Describe the mechanisms, indications, and monitoring of thyroid hormone replacement and antithyroid drug therapy, including management of thyroid storm.
- SO4: Compare glucocorticoid and mineralocorticoid pharmacology, and identify agents used to block adrenocortical hormone synthesis or action.
- SO5: Identify dosing, administration, and monitoring considerations, and explain key medication-safety counseling points to patients, including HPA-axis suppression and the need to taper chronic glucocorticoids.
