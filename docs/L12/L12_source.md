### Autonomic Control of the Lower Urinary Tract — Class
- **Three nerve supplies, three receptor families — the physiologic foundation for every drug in this session.** (S3)
- Parasympathetic M3 contracts the detrusor, sympathetic alpha-1 closes the bladder neck and beta-3 relaxes the reservoir, and somatic nicotinic control holds the external sphincter. (S3 (notes); S4 (notes))
- **Figure 9. Original illustrative diagram (not from Katzung): the parasympathetic, sympathetic, and somatic nervous systems each innervate a different structure of the lower urinary tract through a distinct receptor family —** (S4)
- **the organizing map for every drug class covered this session.** Every class is anchored back to its receptor and anatomic location; Sections 5 and 6 "zoom in" on parts of this map. (S4; S4 (notes))

### Autonomic Control of the Lower Urinary Tract — Prototype + other drugs named
- Oxybutynin (blocks M3 on the detrusor), tamsulosin (blocks alpha1 at the bladder neck/prostate) and mirabegron (stimulates beta3 on the detrusor body) each target one receptor of this map. (S4; S5; S7)

### Autonomic Control of the Lower Urinary Tract — Mechanism of action
- **The parasympathetic pelvic nerve releases acetylcholine, which acts on M3 muscarinic receptors on the detrusor muscle to produce contraction — the primary driver of bladder emptying (voiding).** (S5; SG U6-R11)
- M3 is the dominant muscarinic subtype in the bladder, though M2 receptors are also present in high density (smaller functional contribution normally, may increase in disease). (S5; S5 (notes))
- Chain (Figure 10): pelvic nerve releases acetylcholine → ACh binds M3 on the detrusor → Gq activates phospholipase C; calcium rises → detrusor contracts and the bladder empties. (S6; S6 (notes))
- **M3 is the dominant bladder muscarinic subtype.** **M2 is more abundant but contributes less to contraction.** **Blocking M3 underlies antimuscarinic OAB treatment.** (S6)
- **The sympathetic hypogastric nerve releases norepinephrine, which acts on two different adrenergic receptor subtypes in two different locations, producing two different (and complementary) effects.** (S7; S7 (notes))
- Chain (Figure 11): sympathetic hypogastric nerve releases norepinephrine → alpha-1: outlet closed; beta-3: reservoir relaxed → continence maintained during storage. (S8)
- Alpha1 receptors at the bladder neck, trigone, and proximal urethra: norepinephrine causes contraction, helping maintain continence during storage. (S7; S8; SG U6-R11)
- Beta3 receptors on the detrusor body: norepinephrine causes relaxation, allowing the bladder to fill without a premature urge to void (low pressure). (S7; S8; SG U6-R11)

### Autonomic Control of the Lower Urinary Tract — Indications
- **Because M3 blockade reduces detrusor contraction, antimuscarinic drugs (Section 5) are a logical treatment for conditions of bladder overactivity.** (S5)
- **Both of these receptor subtypes become specific drug targets later in this session: alpha1 blockers for BPH (Section 6), and beta3 agonists for overactive bladder (Section 5).** (S7)

### Autonomic Control of the Lower Urinary Tract — Clinical pearls / "why" hook
- Net storage effect: sympathetic tone keeps the outlet closed (alpha1) while keeping the reservoir relaxed (beta3) — parasympathetic tone is low; both arms work together for continence. During voiding, this pattern reverses. (S7; S8; S8 (notes))
- This is applied Chapter 6: the same muscarinic family that governs other smooth muscle and glands throughout the body — the bladder is simply one more target organ. (S5; S5 (notes))

## Drug class: Overactive Bladder Drugs
Learning objectives covered: SO2, IO6.1, IO6.2, IO6.3, IO6.4, IO6.6

### Overactive Bladder Drugs — Class
- **Two mechanisms, one goal — calming an overactive detrusor to improve storage and reduce urgency.** Antimuscarinics block the detrusor’s muscarinic signal, while beta-3 agonists relax it. (S12; S12 (notes))
- **Figure 13. Original illustrative diagram (not from Katzung): antimuscarinics block the M3-mediated contraction signal, while beta3 agonists enhance the norepinephrine-mediated relaxation signal —** (S16)
- **two distinct mechanisms converging on the same clinical goal of improved bladder storage.** Complementary, not competing — the basis for combination therapy. (S16; S16 (notes))

### Overactive Bladder Drugs — Prototype + other drugs named
- Oxybutynin, tolterodine, solifenacin, darifenacin (antimuscarinics); mirabegron (beta3 agonist). (S13; S15)

### Overactive Bladder Drugs — Mechanism of action
- **Mechanism: block M3 (and, to a lesser extent, M2) muscarinic receptors on the detrusor, reducing involuntary contractions and increasing functional bladder capacity.** (S13; SG U6-R12)
- Antimuscarinic agents differ mainly in receptor selectivity and anticholinergic side-effect burden rather than core mechanism. (S13)
- Chain (Figure 14): drug blocks M3 receptors on the detrusor → fewer involuntary contractions, larger capacity → same blockade in brain, salivary gland and bowel → bladder relaxes, but dry mouth, constipation and confusion follow. (S14; S14 (notes))
- Oxybutynin has the highest CNS anticholinergic burden; mirabegron, a beta-3 agonist, avoids this anticholinergic burden. (S14)
- **Mechanism: stimulate beta3 adrenergic receptors on the detrusor, enhancing smooth muscle relaxation during the storage/filling phase.** Mirabegron is the primary beta3 agonist in clinical use for overactive bladder. (S15; SG U6-R13)

### Overactive Bladder Drugs — Indications
- **Key advantage: lacks the systemic anticholinergic burden of antimuscarinics, making it a preferred option in older adults or patients already on multiple anticholinergic medications.** (S15; SG U6-R13)
- Mirabegron can be used alone or combined with an antimuscarinic for refractory symptoms — complementary (not redundant) mechanisms. (S15; S16 (notes))

### Overactive Bladder Drugs — Adverse effects (boxed warnings first)
- **Adverse effects follow directly from systemic antimuscarinic blockade: dry mouth, constipation, blurred vision, and — importantly — cognitive impairment and confusion, especially in older adults.** (S13; S13 (notes))
- Anticholinergic burden is cumulative across all of a patient’s medications — oxybutynin has the highest CNS anticholinergic burden of this class and is often avoided in older adults or those with dementia risk. (S13; S14; SG U6-R12)
- Mirabegron: can raise blood pressure (monitor in uncontrolled hypertension); headache or nasopharyngitis; less commonly, urinary retention. (S15)

### Overactive Bladder Drugs — Contraindications
- Antimuscarinics: caution/contraindication in narrow-angle glaucoma and significant urinary retention. (S13)

### Overactive Bladder Drugs — Drug interactions
- Mirabegron inhibits CYP2D6. Other anticholinergics (diphenhydramine, tricyclic antidepressants) add to anticholinergic burden; cholinesterase inhibitors (donepezil) oppose antimuscarinics. (S15 (notes); S58; S58 (notes))
- Strong CYP3A4 inhibitors (ketoconazole, clarithromycin) raise solifenacin levels. (S58; S58 (notes))

### Overactive Bladder Drugs — Clinical pearls / "why" hook
- Case: a 78-year-old with mild cognitive impairment and urge incontinence starts oxybutynin; three weeks later, worsening confusion and new difficulty with daily tasks. **What is the most likely explanation, and what change in therapy would you consider?** (S17)
- **Oxybutynin carries a relatively high CNS anticholinergic burden, and in an older adult — especially one with pre-existing cognitive impairment — this can precipitate or worsen confusion.** (S17)
- **Switching to a beta3 agonist (mirabegron), which lacks anticholinergic activity, treats the same overactive bladder symptoms without this risk.** Review other anticholinergics too (medication reconciliation). (S17; S17 (notes))
- The anticholinergic-burden-in-older-adults point is the highest-yield safety point in this section; "no anticholinergic burden" is mirabegron’s single most important advantage. (S13 (notes); S15 (notes))

## Drug class: BPH, Erectile Dysfunction & PDE5 Inhibitors
Learning objectives covered: SO3, SO4, IO6.1, IO6.2, IO6.3, IO6.4, IO6.5, IO6.6

### BPH, Erectile Dysfunction & PDE5 Inhibitors — Class
- **Where genitourinary pharmacology meets the cardiovascular system — alpha1 blockade, nitric oxide signaling, and a life-threatening drug interaction.** Alpha1 blockers relax the prostate; nitric oxide and cGMP drive erection. (S20; S20 (notes))

### BPH, Erectile Dysfunction & PDE5 Inhibitors — Prototype + other drugs named
- Tamsulosin, silodosin, doxazosin, terazosin (alpha1 blockers); finasteride, dutasteride (5-ARIs); sildenafil, tadalafil, vardenafil (PDE5 inhibitors). (S21; S23; S26)

### BPH, Erectile Dysfunction & PDE5 Inhibitors — Mechanism of action
- **Mechanism: block alpha1 receptors at the bladder neck, prostatic smooth muscle, and proximal urethra, relaxing outflow resistance and improving urinary flow in benign prostatic hyperplasia.** (S21)
- **Figure 14. Original illustrative diagram (not from Katzung): sympathetic norepinephrine normally contracts bladder neck and prostatic smooth muscle via alpha1 receptors, narrowing the outflow tract;** (S22)
- **alpha1 blockers relax this same muscle to improve urinary flow — while the same receptor family elsewhere mediates vascular tone.** (S22; S22 (notes))
- **Mechanism: block 5-alpha-reductase, the enzyme that converts testosterone to the more potent dihydrotestosterone (DHT) — the same mechanism introduced in Session 1 for hirsutism and androgenetic alopecia.** (S23; S23 (notes))
- 5-ARI chain (Figure 15): testosterone is converted by 5-alpha-reductase → the enzyme makes DHT → DHT drives prostate growth → enzyme blocked, DHT falls, prostate shrinks slowly. (S24; S24 (notes))
- **Figure 16. Original illustrative diagram (not from Katzung): non-adrenergic, noncholinergic (NANC) nerves release nitric oxide, raising cGMP and relaxing corpus cavernosum smooth muscle;** (S25)
- **PDE5 normally terminates this signal by breaking down cGMP, and nitrates independently raise NO/cGMP systemically — the mechanistic basis for the absolute nitrate/PDE5-inhibitor contraindication.** (S25)
- **Mechanism: block phosphodiesterase type 5 (PDE5), the enzyme that normally breaks down cGMP, prolonging smooth muscle relaxation in the corpus cavernosum and sustaining erectile function (Figure 16).** (S26)
- Chain (Figure 17): sexual stimulation → NANC nerves release nitric oxide → guanylate cyclase makes cGMP → cGMP relaxes corpus cavernosum smooth muscle → erection. PDE5 breaks down cGMP and ends the signal. (S27)
- Sildenafil, tadalafil and vardenafil block PDE5 and prolong the signal; an erection still needs sexual stimulation to start the pathway. (S27; S27 (notes))

### BPH, Erectile Dysfunction & PDE5 Inhibitors — Indications
- Tamsulosin, silodosin (more uroselective, less vascular effect) vs. doxazosin, terazosin (less selective, more likely to lower blood pressure systemically). (S21; S21 (notes); SG U6-R14)
- Finasteride, dutasteride: in BPH, reducing DHT shrinks prostatic tissue over time, improving urinary symptoms. (S23)
- Onset: 5-ARI benefit typically takes 3–6 months, unlike the rapid onset of alpha1 blockers — the classes are frequently combined (alpha1 blocker for rapid relief, 5-ARI for long-term disease modification). Alpha1 blockers act in days; 5-ARIs take months. (S23; S24; SG U6-R15)
- PDE5 agents: sildenafil, tadalafil (longer half-life, allows more flexible dosing), vardenafil. (S26)
- [TB] Finasteride is moderately effective in reducing prostate size in BPH; 1 mg/d is approved for early male pattern baldness. (p. 781)

### BPH, Erectile Dysfunction & PDE5 Inhibitors — Adverse effects (boxed warnings first)
- **Adverse effects: orthostatic hypotension and a "first-dose effect" (pronounced hypotension with the first dose) — because alpha1 receptors also mediate vascular smooth muscle tone, not just bladder neck tone.** (S21; SG U6-R14)
- Floppy iris syndrome is a recognized intraoperative complication during cataract surgery in patients on alpha1 blockers — relevant to preoperative medication reconciliation. (S21; S21 (notes))
- **Adverse effects: decreased libido, erectile dysfunction, and — a key counseling point — these agents lower PSA levels by roughly half, which must be accounted for when interpreting PSA values in patients on long-term therapy.** (S23; S24; SG U6-R15)
- PDE5 common adverse effects: headache, flushing, dyspepsia, and visual disturbances (blue-tinged vision, more notable with sildenafil). (S26)

### BPH, Erectile Dysfunction & PDE5 Inhibitors — Contraindications
- **Absolute contraindication: concurrent use with any nitrate — both raise NO/cGMP signaling, and together they can cause profound, life-threatening hypotension.** (S26)
- Why absolute rather than relative: nitrates and PDE5 inhibitors independently raise NO/cGMP signaling, and together can produce profound, life-threatening hypotension — pharmacodynamic and severe regardless of dose. (SG U6-R16)
- **This mirrors the identical nitrate/PDE5-inhibitor warning from Unit 5's angina content, now from the opposite side of the interaction.** Riociguat is also contraindicated with PDE5 inhibitors. (S26; S26 (notes); S58)
- 5-ARI pregnancy caution: finasteride/dutasteride can cause ambiguous genitalia in a male fetus; pregnant partners should avoid handling crushed or broken tablets. (S23; S24)

### BPH, Erectile Dysfunction & PDE5 Inhibitors — Drug interactions
- Use caution with alpha1 blockers and PDE5 inhibitors together — additive hypotension, less severe than with nitrates, still warrants spacing doses or careful monitoring. (S26; S29)
- Nitrate + PDE5 inhibitor (Figure 18): nitrate raises NO and cGMP systemically → PDE5 inhibitor stops cGMP breakdown → together, vasodilation becomes profound → severe hypotension and syncope. Never combine. (S29; S29 (notes))
- Antihypertensives: additive hypotension with alpha-1 blockers. Strong CYP3A4 inhibitors (ketoconazole, clarithromycin) raise PDE5 inhibitor levels. (S58; S58 (notes))

### BPH, Erectile Dysfunction & PDE5 Inhibitors — Pharmacokinetics
- [TB] Finasteride lowers DHT within 8 hours, lasting about 24 hours; dutasteride has a slow onset and a much longer half-life. (p. 781)

### BPH, Erectile Dysfunction & PDE5 Inhibitors — Monitoring
- PSA falls about half on long-term 5-ARI therapy; read PSA accordingly. (S24)

### BPH, Erectile Dysfunction & PDE5 Inhibitors — Clinical pearls / "why" hook
- **This is the exact same receptor family targeted by alpha1-blocking antihypertensives and central agents discussed in Unit 5 — the difference here is clinical intent (urinary flow vs. blood pressure), not a different drug class.** (S21; S21 (notes); S22 (notes))
- Finasteride/dutasteride here are a deliberate callback to Session 1: same mechanism, same drugs, different clinical application. (S23 (notes))
- Figure 16 applies the Chapter 6 non-adrenergic, noncholinergic neuron reading; the nitrate warning is the highest-stakes safety point in the unit — students saw it from the nitrate side in Unit 5, today from the PDE5 side. (S25 (notes); S26 (notes))
- Case: a 64-year-old with stable angina on isosorbide mononitrate asks for an ED prescription — a friend takes sildenafil "with no problems." Ask yourself: **What should the PA do, and why?** (S28)
- **PDE5 inhibitors are absolutely contraindicated with any nitrate — both raise NO/cGMP signaling, and the combination can cause severe, life-threatening hypotension.** (S28)
- **This prescription should not be written while the patient remains on a nitrate; the conversation should include alternative angina management or alternative ED options that do not involve this interaction, in consultation with his cardiologist.** (S28; S28 (notes))
- This case pulls a Unit 5 drug (isosorbide mononitrate) into a Unit 6 decision, making the shared NO-cGMP mechanism unmistakable. (S28 (notes))
- Drug names reveal the class: **-osin** alpha1 blocker (tamsulosin, doxazosin, terazosin, silodosin; opens the outlet); **-terodine** antimuscarinic (tolterodine, fesoterodine; dampens detrusor M3 signal); **-fenacin** antimuscarinic (solifenacin, darifenacin; same class, same target). (S32; S32 (notes))
- **oxybutynin / trospium** antimuscarinic (older, higher CNS burden); **-begron** beta-3 agonist (mirabegron, vibegron; relaxes the reservoir); **-afil** PDE5 inhibitor (sildenafil, tadalafil, vardenafil; keeps cGMP high). (S32)

### Putting It All Together: GU Toolkit — How it works
- Session 1’s gonadal hormone pharmacology (Ch. 40) and PCOS (Ch. 41 overlap) and Session 2’s autonomic bladder pharmacology (Ch. 6) are distinct but connect through shared classes — 5-alpha-reductase inhibitors appear in both sessions. (S38)
- The lower urinary tract is governed by three nerve supplies and three receptor families (Figure 9): parasympathetic/M3, sympathetic/alpha1 and beta3, and somatic/nicotinic. (S38)
- Antimuscarinics and beta3 agonists treat overactive bladder through complementary mechanisms — blocking contraction vs. enhancing relaxation — with anticholinergic burden the key safety differentiator in older adults. (S38)
- Alpha1 blockers and 5-ARIs treat BPH through different timescales and mechanisms and are often combined; alpha1 blockade overlaps directly with Unit 5’s antihypertensive pharmacology. (S38)
- The NO-cGMP pathway (Figure 16) underlies both erectile function and nitrate angina therapy (Unit 5) — therapeutic in one context, dangerous in combination in another: one of the highest-stakes safety points in the course. (S38)
- Close by naming the cross-unit integration: genitourinary pharmacology shares receptor families and drug classes with cardiovascular pharmacology covered earlier in the course. (S38 (notes))

### Putting It All Together: GU Toolkit — Exam traps
- Integrated case: a PDE5 inhibitor should not be prescribed while this patient remains on isosorbide mononitrate — both drugs independently raise NO/cGMP signaling; the combination can cause profound, life-threatening hypotension. (SG U6-S)
- This is an absolute contraindication, and any ED treatment decision must involve his cardiologist and consideration of whether his anginal nitrate therapy can be modified first. (SG U6-S)
- Even without the nitrate, tamsulosin and a PDE5 inhibitor both lower blood pressure (alpha1 blockade and NO-cGMP potentiation) — additive orthostatic hypotension warrants spacing doses or close monitoring. (SG U6-S)
- His wife’s new vaginal bleeding on tamoxifen should not be dismissed — tamoxifen’s endometrial agonism raises hyperplasia/cancer risk; she needs prompt gynecologic evaluation. (SG U6-S)
