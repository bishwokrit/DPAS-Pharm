Units

Readings & objectives

Unit 1Introduction to PharmacologyExam Fri, Oct 30

Reading: Katzung Ch. 1–5

  • IO1.1 Define and describe fundamental pharmacologic concepts, including pharmacokinetics, pharmacodynamics, drug-receptor interactions, dose-response relationships, therapeutic index, half-life, and steady state.
  • IO1.2 Describe the processes of drug absorption, distribution, metabolism, and excretion and factors that influence these processes.
  • IO1.3 Explain how patient-specific factors, including age, pregnancy, renal and hepatic function, and pharmacogenetic variation, may affect medication response.
  • IO1.4 Identify common types and mechanisms of adverse drug reactions, drug-drug interactions, and drug-disease interactions.
  • IO1.5 Interpret common components of medication orders and prescriptions, including drug name, dose, route, frequency, and duration.
Unit 2MusculoskeletalExam Fri, Nov 6

Reading: Katzung Ch. 27, 36, 42

  • IO2.1 Identify major pharmacologic classes used in the management of musculoskeletal conditions and describe their mechanisms of action.
  • IO2.2 Describe the indications, contraindications, adverse effects, and clinically significant interactions associated with medications used to treat musculoskeletal conditions.
  • IO2.3 Compare pharmacologic agents within and across therapeutic classes to distinguish clinically relevant differences in efficacy, safety, and use.
  • IO2.4 Select appropriate pharmacologic therapy for common musculoskeletal conditions based on clinical presentation and patient-specific factors.
  • IO2.5 Identify appropriate dosing, administration, and monitoring considerations for medications used in musculoskeletal conditions.
  • IO2.6 Explain important medication-use and safety information to patients receiving pharmacotherapy for musculoskeletal.

Outcomes

Course goal & outcomes

By the end of this course, students will integrate foundational pharmacologic principles with therapeutic reasoning to select, evaluate, and communicate safe, effective, evidence-based pharmacotherapy for common conditions encountered in clinical practice.

  1. Explain foundational principles of pharmacology and their relationship to medication effects.
  2. Compare pharmacologic agents based on their therapeutic and pharmacologic characteristics.
  3. Apply pharmacologic knowledge to select safe and effective pharmacotherapy for common medical conditions.
  4. Apply principles of safe medication prescribing and management.
  5. Communicate medication-related information effectively to patients and caregivers.
