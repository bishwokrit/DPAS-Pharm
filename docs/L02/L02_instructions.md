
## 4. Questions to ask the notebook
1. Compare Phase I and Phase II metabolism. Which enzymes, which reactions, and what is the result?
2. What happens to a substrate drug's level with a CYP inhibitor vs. an inducer, and what is the clinical risk of each?
3. Walk me through the half-life worked example (t½ 6 h, 100 mg/L start) for each half-life.
4. Why does chronic kidney disease prolong half-life and raise drug levels? What should I adjust?
5. What are loading and maintenance doses based on, and what does a loading dose do?
6. Match each pharmacogenomic variant to its drug: CYP2D6, CYP2C19, TPMT, HLA-B*5801.
7. List the six items on the clinical decision-making checklist.
8. How do filtration, secretion, and reabsorption differ in the kidney?
9. How would I confirm that a new drug is a competitive antagonist of a home medication?

Learning objectives for reference:
- IO1.1: Define and describe fundamental pharmacologic concepts, including pharmacokinetics, pharmacodynamics, drug-receptor interactions, dose-response relationships, therapeutic index, half-life, and steady state.
- IO1.2: Describe the processes of drug absorption, distribution, metabolism, and excretion and factors that influence these processes.
- IO1.3: Explain how patient-specific factors, including age, pregnancy, renal and hepatic function, and pharmacogenetic variation, may affect medication response.
- IO1.4: Identify common types and mechanisms of adverse drug reactions, drug-drug interactions, and drug-disease interactions.
- IO1.5: Interpret common components of medication orders and prescriptions, including drug name, dose, route, frequency, and duration.
- SO1: Explain drug biotransformation (Phase I/II metabolism) and the clinical impact of CYP450 enzyme inhibition and induction.
- SO2: Explain mechanisms of drug excretion and how renal function affects dosing.
- SO3: Calculate and apply basic pharmacokinetic parameters (half-life, steady state, clearance).
- SO4: Describe how pharmacogenomic variation can alter drug response.
- SO5: Apply a structured framework to clinical prescribing decisions.
