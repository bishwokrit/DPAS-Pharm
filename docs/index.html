High-yield from this lecture

  • PK = what the body does to the drug (ADME); PD = what the drug does to the body (receptors & effect).
  • Efficacy = ideal trial conditions; effectiveness = everyday practice (adherence, comorbidities).
  • Drug → target → signal transduction → response.
  • Potency = dose needed (EC₅₀, lower = more potent); efficacy = Emax (ceiling).

1Drug Metabolism & CYP450 Interactions2Drug Excretion & Renal Dosing3PK Calculations: Half-Life, Steady State & Clearance4Pharmacogenomics5Putting It All Together: Clinical Decision-Making

High-yield from this lecture

  • Phase I (CYP450): oxidation, reduction, hydrolysis. Phase II: conjugation → water-soluble.
  • Kidney = dominant elimination route; filtration (free drug), secretion (transporters), reabsorption (lipophilic, un-ionized).
  • t½ = time for plasma concentration to fall 50%; set by clearance + Vd.
  • Pharmacogenomics = genetic variation changing drug response (metabolism, transport, target, ADR risk).

Musculoskeletal Pharmacology — Session 1

Unit 2 · Session 1 · Tuesday, October 27 · Katzung Ch. 27, 36, 42

Open study guideFocus quizListen
1Musculoskeletal Pain & the Inflammatory Cascade2Acetaminophen3NSAIDs & COX-2 Selective Inhibition4Osteoarthritis, Rheumatoid Arthritis & DMARDs

High-yield from this lecture

  • Injury → mediators → nociceptor sensitization → pain.
  • Acetaminophen treats pain and fever but is not a reliable anti-inflammatory agent; its overdose toxicity is mediated by NAPQI accumulation.
  • COX-1 = housekeeping (gastric protection, renal blood flow); COX-2 = inflammation.
  • OA = degenerative → pain control; RA = autoimmune → suppress disease activity.

Musculoskeletal Pharmacology — Session 2

Unit 2 · Session 2 · Thursday, October 29 · Katzung Ch. 27, 36, 42

Open study guideFocus quizListen
1Gout: Acute Flares & Urate-Lowering Therapy2Skeletal Muscle Relaxants3Bone Pharmacology & Osteoporosis4Putting It All Together: Musculoskeletal Toolkit

High-yield from this lecture

  • Acute gout treatment and chronic urate-lowering therapy are different goals, started at different times.
  • Central (cyclobenzaprine, tizanidine, baclofen) vs. peripheral (dantrolene — muscle; botulinum — junction).
  • Bone pharmacology is fundamentally about the balance between osteoblast and osteoclast activity.
  • Five takeaways: pain ≠ inflammation; NSAIDs = GI/renal/CV risk; DMARDs change RA; gout = acute vs. chronic; bone = osteoblast vs. osteoclast.

GI & Nutrition Pharmacology — Session 1

Unit 3 · Session 1 · Tuesday, November 3 · Katzung Ch. 62, 65

Open study guideFocus quizListen
1Acid-Peptic Disease & Gastric Acid Secretion2Antacids, H2 Blockers & Proton Pump Inhibitors3Mucosal Protectants & H. pylori Eradication4Antiemetics

High-yield from this lecture

  • Acid-peptic disease reflects an imbalance between mucosal defense and aggressive factors (acid, pepsin, H. pylori, NSAIDs).
  • PPIs block the parietal cell’s final common pathway and are the most potent antisecretory agents, but carry real long-term risks (B12 deficiency, hypomagnesemia, fracture, C. difficile).
  • H. pylori eradication requires multi-drug regimens (triple or bismuth quadruple therapy), not acid suppression alone.
  • Antiemetics are organized by the receptor pathway they block — serotonin (5-HT3), dopamine/neurokinin (CTZ), or histamine/acetylcholine (vestibular) — all converging on the vomiting center.

GI & Nutrition Pharmacology — Session 2

Unit 3 · Session 2 · Thursday, November 5 · Katzung Ch. 62, 65

Open study guideFocus quizListen
1Laxatives & Antidiarrheals2Inflammatory Bowel Disease3Nutrition, Vitamins & Dietary Supplements4Putting It All Together: GI & Nutrition Toolkit

High-yield from this lecture

  • Four laxative buckets: bulk-forming (slowest, safest), osmotic (fast; PEG first-line), stimulant (short-term), secretagogue (IBS-C).
  • IBD therapy follows a step-wise escalation matched to disease severity — from 5-ASA agents to biologics/small molecules.
  • Nutrition and dietary supplements are not inert — vitamin status, drug-nutrient interactions, and herbal supplements can all meaningfully change drug therapy outcomes.
  • Five takeaways: pathway targets · PPI risks · IBD step therapy · shared biologic risk · supplements matter.

Endocrine Pharmacotherapy — Session 1

Unit 4 · Session 1 · Tuesday, November 10 · Katzung Ch. 37, 38, 39, 40, 41, 42

Open study guideFocus quizListen
1Pituitary: GH, Prolactin & ADH2Thyroid & Antithyroid Drugs3Adrenocorticosteroids & Antagonists

High-yield from this lecture

  • Acromegaly: octreotide/lanreotide or pegvisomant (monitor IGF-1); prolactinoma: cabergoline; DI: desmopressin (hyponatremia); SIADH: fluid restriction, then tolvaptan.
  • Levothyroxine: empty stomach, separate calcium/iron/antacids by 4 h, TSH at 6–8 weeks, start low in older/cardiac patients.
  • Chronic steroids suppress CRH/ACTH → taper; abrupt stop → adrenal crisis → IV hydrocortisone + fluids.

Endocrine Pharmacotherapy — Session 2

Unit 4 · Session 2 · Thursday, November 12 · Katzung Ch. 37, 38, 39, 40, 41, 42

Open study guideFocus quizListen
1Gonadal Hormones & Inhibitors2Insulin3Non-Insulin Glucose-Lowering Drugs4Parathyroid Disorders5Putting It All Together: Endocrine Toolkit

High-yield from this lecture

  • No estrogen with prior VTE/stroke, smoker ≥ 35, migraine with aura, estrogen-sensitive cancer, uncontrolled hypertension → progestin-only.
  • Insulin’s main risk is hypoglycemia; beta-blockers mask its warning signs; only regular insulin goes IV.
  • Metformin first-line (no weight gain, low hypoglycemia); avoid eGFR < 30; hold for contrast; B12.
  • High PTH (CKD) → cinacalcet, watch hypocalcemia; low PTH (post-thyroidectomy) → calcitriol + calcium, watch hypercalcemia.

Renal Pharmacotherapeutics — Session 1

Unit 5 · Session 1 · Tuesday, November 17 · Katzung Ch. 15, 3

Open study guideFocus quizListen
1Renal Dosing & Diuretic Sites2Loop & Thiazide Diuretics3Potassium-Sparing, CAI & Osmotic Diuretics

High-yield from this lecture

  • Falling GFR → accumulation → toxicity: cut the maintenance dose, stretch the interval, or both; check CrCl first.
  • Loop = NKCC2, thick ascending limb, most potent, ↑ Ca²⁺ excretion, ototoxicity. Thiazide = NCC, DCT, first-line HTN, ↓ Ca²⁺ excretion.
  • Potassium-sparing: aldosterone antagonists (spironolactone, eplerenone) and ENaC blockers (amiloride, triamterene); hyperkalemia worsens with ACEi/ARB or CKD.

Renal Pharmacotherapeutics — Session 2

Unit 5 · Session 2 · Thursday, November 19 · Katzung Ch. 15, 3

Open study guideFocus quizListen
1RAAS Blockers2Other Antihypertensives & Resistant Hypertension3Nitrates, Angina & the Triple Whammy4Putting It All Together: Renal Toolkit

High-yield from this lecture

  • ACEi/ARB first-line in proteinuric CKD: efferent dilation lowers intraglomerular pressure and proteinuria.
  • Non-DHP CCB + beta-blocker → bradycardia/AV block; DHP + beta-blocker is fine.
  • Nitrates: NO → cGMP → venodilation, ↓ preload; nitrate-free interval; never with PDE5 inhibitors.
  • Five takeaways: two targetable maps · ACEi/ARB renoprotection · CCB overlap · triple whammy · HTN–CKD cycle.

Genitourinary & Reproductive Pharmacology — Session 1

Unit 6 · Session 1 · Tuesday, November 24 · Katzung Ch. 40, 41

Open study guideFocus quizListen
1Estrogens, Progestins & Contraception2SERMs, Aromatase Inhibitors & Androgens3PCOS & the Reproductive-Metabolic Overlap

High-yield from this lecture

  • Estrogen = VTE (smoking, age > 35, VTE history); unopposed estrogen = endometrial cancer → always pair with a progestin if the uterus is intact.
  • Tamoxifen: breast antagonist, endometrial agonist → bleeding needs evaluation; raloxifene: endometrium neutral; both VTE.
  • PCOS = insulin resistance ↔ hyperandrogenism cycle; metformin (metabolic) + combined contraception (cycles, hirsutism) are usually combined.

Genitourinary & Reproductive Pharmacology — Session 2

Unit 6 · Session 2 · Tuesday, December 1 · Katzung Ch. 40, 41

Open study guideFocus quizListen
1Autonomic Control of the Lower Urinary Tract2Overactive Bladder Drugs3BPH, Erectile Dysfunction & PDE5 Inhibitors4Putting It All Together: GU Toolkit

High-yield from this lecture

  • M3 (detrusor) contracts to void; alpha1 (neck) closes and beta3 (detrusor) relaxes to store.
  • OAB in an older adult or anyone on other anticholinergics → mirabegron; oxybutynin has the highest CNS burden.
  • BPH: alpha1 blocker (days; orthostasis, first dose) + 5-ARI (3–6 months; PSA halved) — often together.
  • GU receptor families recur in the vasculature: alpha1 blockers lower BP; PDE5 inhibitors and nitrates share cGMP.
