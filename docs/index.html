High-yield from this lecture

  • PK = what the body does to the drug (ADME); PD = what the drug does to the body (receptors & effect).
  • Efficacy = ideal trial conditions; effectiveness = everyday practice (adherence, comorbidities).
  • Drug → target → signal transduction → response.
  • Potency = dose needed (EC₅₀, lower = more potent); efficacy = Emax (ceiling).

High-yield from this lecture

  • Phase I (CYP450): oxidation, reduction, hydrolysis. Phase II: conjugation → water-soluble.
  • Kidney = dominant elimination route; filtration (free drug), secretion (transporters), reabsorption (lipophilic, un-ionized).
  • t½ = time for plasma concentration to fall 50%; set by clearance + Vd.
  • Pharmacogenomics = genetic variation changing drug response (metabolism, transport, target, ADR risk).

Musculoskeletal Pharmacology — Session 1

Unit 2 · Session 1 · Tuesday, October 27 · Katzung Ch. 27, 36, 42

Open study guideFocus quizListen
1Musculoskeletal Pain & the Inflammatory Cascade2Acetaminophen3NSAIDs & COX-2 Selective Inhibition4Osteoarthritis, Rheumatoid Arthritis & DMARDs

High-yield from this lecture

  • Injury → mediators → nociceptor sensitization → pain.
  • Acetaminophen treats pain and fever but is not a reliable anti-inflammatory agent; its overdose toxicity is mediated by NAPQI accumulation.
  • COX-1 = housekeeping (gastric protection, renal blood flow); COX-2 = inflammation.
  • OA = degenerative → pain control; RA = autoimmune → suppress disease activity.

Musculoskeletal Pharmacology — Session 2

Unit 2 · Session 2 · Thursday, October 29 · Katzung Ch. 27, 36, 42

Open study guideFocus quizListen
1Gout: Acute Flares & Urate-Lowering Therapy2Skeletal Muscle Relaxants3Bone Pharmacology & Osteoporosis4Putting It All Together: Musculoskeletal Toolkit

High-yield from this lecture

  • Acute gout treatment and chronic urate-lowering therapy are different goals, started at different times.
  • Central (cyclobenzaprine, tizanidine, baclofen) vs. peripheral (dantrolene — muscle; botulinum — junction).
  • Bone pharmacology is fundamentally about the balance between osteoblast and osteoclast activity.
  • Five takeaways: pain ≠ inflammation; NSAIDs = GI/renal/CV risk; DMARDs change RA; gout = acute vs. chronic; bone = osteoblast vs. osteoclast.

GI & Nutrition Pharmacology — Session 1

Unit 3 · Session 1 · Tuesday, November 3 · Katzung Ch. 62, 65

Open study guideFocus quizListen
1Acid-Peptic Disease & Gastric Acid Secretion2Antacids, H2 Blockers & Proton Pump Inhibitors3Mucosal Protectants & H. pylori Eradication4Antiemetics

High-yield from this lecture

  • Acid-peptic disease reflects an imbalance between mucosal defense and aggressive factors (acid, pepsin, H. pylori, NSAIDs).
  • PPIs block the parietal cell’s final common pathway and are the most potent antisecretory agents, but carry real long-term risks (B12 deficiency, hypomagnesemia, fracture, C. difficile).
  • H. pylori eradication requires multi-drug regimens (triple or bismuth quadruple therapy), not acid suppression alone.
  • Antiemetics are organized by the receptor pathway they block — serotonin (5-HT3), dopamine/neurokinin (CTZ), or histamine/acetylcholine (vestibular) — all converging on the vomiting center.

GI & Nutrition Pharmacology — Session 2

Unit 3 · Session 2 · Thursday, November 5 · Katzung Ch. 62, 65

Open study guideFocus quizListen
1Laxatives & Antidiarrheals2Inflammatory Bowel Disease3Nutrition, Vitamins & Dietary Supplements4Putting It All Together: GI & Nutrition Toolkit

High-yield from this lecture

  • Four laxative buckets: bulk-forming (slowest, safest), osmotic (fast; PEG first-line), stimulant (short-term), secretagogue (IBS-C).
  • IBD therapy follows a step-wise escalation matched to disease severity — from 5-ASA agents to biologics/small molecules.
  • Nutrition and dietary supplements are not inert — vitamin status, drug-nutrient interactions, and herbal supplements can all meaningfully change drug therapy outcomes.
  • Five takeaways: pathway targets · PPI risks · IBD step therapy · shared biologic risk · supplements matter.
